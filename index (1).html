A small satellite that decides what to send, and when.

SomaiyaSat is a PocketQube, a satellite about the size of a coffee mug. It gets only a few short passes over a ground station each day. An onboard scheduler chooses which data goes down in each pass: health data first, then images, then voice.

The problem

Little time

A satellite in low Earth orbit is in range of one ground station for only a few minutes per pass. Everything has to fit in that window.

Little power

Solar panels are tiny and the battery drains during eclipse. Every transmission costs energy that is hard to get back.

Too much to send

Telemetry, SSTV pictures, Codec2 voice and M17 data all compete for the same radio. Manual control from the ground is too slow.

How the system is built

Four inputs feed one decision-maker. The decision-maker controls the radio modes and talks to the ground through a single transceiver. The model is deliberately small (a decision tree or tiny network) so it fits on a low-power microcontroller.

    Safety and rules

    Never lose contact

    If the AI output looks wrong, a watchdog switches to a fixed rule-based order. Try the fault switch in the simulator.

    Humans can override

    Ground operators can send commands that override the scheduler, and safe mode limits what it can do.

    Explainable choices

    Each decision is logged with its reason, so the ground team can debug it. The log above is a small version of this.
